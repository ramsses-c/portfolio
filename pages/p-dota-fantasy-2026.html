The goal

Reach the top 1,500 players in the Dota 2 Fantasy and Predictions event. The approximate cutoff was around 31,000 points, while the 95th percentile sat at just 20,743 points. Our goal was therefore ambitious, but the model still had to account for a lot of uncertainty: player performance, game count, matchups and, inevitably, some good old-fashioned luck.

The problem

Build a Fantasy roster of 2 cores, 1 mid and 2 supports, then maximize the points they would earn throughout the tournament. To do that, I combined player performance, games played, hero selections, roles, team matchups, tournament stage and Valve's scoring rules.

Rather than simply picking the strongest players, I turned that data into expected Fantasy value and used it to find the combination most likely to maximize the final score. Bracket predictions were handled separately, since predicting which team wins a series is a very different problem from predicting which players will score the most.

The result

The results were... mixed.

On one hand we have the Group Stage where the Fantasy roster reached the 94.1 percentile and the Swiss predictions ended up at 9/16 correct

The Main Event predictions, however, got absolutely destroyed at 3/14 correct, but the Fantasy strategy worked: the roster reached the 99.9th percentile, contributing to a final result of 25,732 points and the 98th percentile overall.
